Method, setup & the ASX catches

Map and list

The chart puts the multiple on the vertical axis — cheap sits low, inside the shaded value zone under 8×. Left to right is net debt measured in years of operating earnings, so left is safer: the bottom-left corner is cheap companies that could pay their debt off quickly, and anything left of the "no net debt" line is sitting on net cash. Bubble size is market cap, colour is sector.

Tap a sector chip to hide that sector from both views; tap again to bring it back, or hit All to reset. Star a company — on its row, or after tapping its bubble — and it joins the To look into shortlist under the chart, sorted cheapest first. Inside every company there is a Research row linking straight to its Yahoo page, ASX announcements, chart and news. Swipe a row left and confirm to delete it — an undo appears for a few seconds. The Banks & REITs chip shows or hides the financial names, which never rank either way.

Installing it on your phone

In Safari, tap Share → Add to Home Screen. It then opens like a real app — its own icon, no browser bars, and it works with no signal, since the whole screen runs off data already on the phone and only needs the network to refresh prices.

Back up before you install. iPhone gives a home-screen app its own private storage, separate from Safari's, so the installed copy opens empty the first time — your companies are still in Safari, not lost. Tap Back up now in Settings first, install, then tap Restore from cloud inside the installed app and everything comes across. After that the two are independent: work in the installed one, and treat Safari as a spare.

Setting up auto-update

  1. Open script.google.com and start a new project
  2. Paste in the proxy script, save, then Deploy → New deployment → Web app
  3. Execute as Me, access Anyone. Authorise it — you will get an "unverified app" warning, that is your own script talking
  4. Copy the /exec URL into Settings above and hit Test connection

Yahoo has no public API, so the proxy uses their internal endpoints. They change them without warning. If quotes stop coming through, add ?fn=clearcache to your exec URL and load it once — a stale crumb is usually the problem.

Real company logos

Avatars in the List and Favourites views can show a real company logo instead of a coloured initial. This is resolved by your proxy script, not the browser — see apps-script-logo-addon.gs.txt in the project files for the snippet to paste into your existing script and redeploy. Once installed, logos fill in automatically the next time you fetch prices or financials for a company. The Website field inside a company is a fallback the browser can use on its own for anything the proxy doesn't recognise.

Cloud backup

Back up now sends your full company list — tickers, financials, notes, portfolio lots, price history — plus your view settings to a private Google Sheet that your own script creates in your own Drive, not a third party. This needs the same proxy script as everything else, with one addition: see apps-script-sync-addon.gs.txt in the project files for the snippet to paste into your existing script and redeploy. No spreadsheet setup needed — the first backup creates it for you. It's manual both ways: nothing uploads or restores unless you tap the button, and restoring replaces everything currently on this device.

Value-zone alerts

Star a company and it's opted in to a daily email the moment its Acquirer's Multiple newly drops under 8× — no separate settings screen, since starring is already "I'm tracking this." The check runs on your proxy's own schedule, not in this browser tab, so it still catches it while you're not in the app. It depends on cloud backup: the checker only ever sees your last backup, so back up at least once, and again any time you star or unstar a company, for the alert to reflect it. See apps-script-alerts-addon.gs.txt in the project files for the code and setup — paste it into the same script (no dispatcher edit needed this time, since it runs on a schedule instead of a URL request), run checkValueZoneAlerts once by hand to grant it permission to send mail, then add a time-driven trigger for it from the clock icon in the Apps Script sidebar. You're only emailed the first time a company crosses under 8×, not every day it stays there; if it later falls out and comes back in, that counts as a fresh crossing.

Portfolio

Open any company and add a buy — date, shares, price paid — under its Portfolio section. Add as many as you like for the same company; the app averages your cost across all of them. Once a company has at least one buy, it shows up in the Portfolio tab with its current value and unrealised profit or loss, and the tab's summary totals everything you hold. Nothing here is sent anywhere — it's stored the same way as the rest of your data.

Selling takes the whole position in one go — that's how this strategy exits, so there's no share count to retype, just the price. The sale is recorded alongside your buys rather than erasing them, so realised profit survives and the position stays visible under Closed with its result. If the position is in profit and has dropped out of the ranking, you'll be told the date it reaches a year and a day first; you can still sell anyway.

The today figures need one more thing from your proxy: previousClose added to the price-quote response. Without it, the app still shows total value and all-time profit/loss, just not the day's move.

Monthly plan

Under More. It takes the ranking and your holdings and works out the two things the strategy actually asks each month: what to buy, and what has stopped being cheap.

Buying. While you own fewer companies than you're aiming for, the month's contribution opens new positions at $500 each rather than dusting every name — the ASX won't accept a first purchase in a stock below roughly $500, so $1,000 opens two positions a month, not twenty slivers. Once all the positions are open the same money goes wherever the weights have drifted lightest, which keeps things equal weight without ever selling to rebalance.

Selling. The only trigger is a holding dropping out of the target — never how long you've held it, never how far it has run. It could be nobody one quarter and five the next. Where a dropped holding is in profit, the plan recommends holding to a year and a day so the taxable gain is halved, and shows the date. At a loss there's no discount to wait for, so it says sell.

Holdings that don't rank at all — a bank, or anything missing figures — are listed separately rather than counted as exits, because a missing multiple isn't the same as a company that got expensive. Anything carrying Check figures is flagged too: a stale multiple can drop a company out of the ranking on its own.

Fetch all financials

Works through the whole list one company at a time, roughly 1.3 seconds apart so Yahoo does not throttle you. It saves after every company, so stopping it or losing the page costs you nothing — start it again and it picks up where it left off. It skips banks and REITs, and skips anything already pulled in the last month. Come results season, run it again and it refreshes the lot. Smaller companies often come back empty — Yahoo's coverage thins out down the list. Those are the ones to paste by hand.

Force refresh all, next to it, ignores the one-month gate entirely and re-fetches every company regardless of when it was last pulled. That's the one to reach for after changing something in your proxy script — the normal Fetch all financials would otherwise skip everything it already has, including the companies whose data was wrong.

Pasting from Yahoo

Open au.finance.yahoo.com/quote/BHP.AX/financials/, select the table, copy, and paste it into the box inside any company. Same for the balance sheet tab. The parser picks out what it recognises and leaves the rest alone. Yahoo prints everything in thousands — that is handled. What is not handled is which impairments were genuinely one-off, so still read the annual report before you trust a number.

The currency trap

A handful of Australian companies — BHP, Rio, South32, Ansell, Woodside among them — report in USD while trading in AUD. Mixing a USD earnings figure with an AUD market cap produces a multiple that means nothing, so the app converts instead: every reported line item — debt, cash, earnings, revenue — is multiplied into the currency the share price is quoted in before anything is calculated. The share count needs no conversion, and neither does the price.

The rate comes down with your prices, through the same proxy call — Yahoo treats a currency pair as an ordinary ticker, so nothing needed adding to your script. Until there's a rate, those companies are left out of the ranking entirely rather than ranked on a number built from two currencies. Open one and you can type the rate by hand instead; it's stored per currency pair, so setting it once for USD fixes every USD reporter at once.

For any of this to happen the app has to know which currency a company reports in. The reliable answer comes from Fetch financials: Yahoo attaches the reporting currency to every figure it sends, so pulling a company's financials sets it correctly and keeps it correct as companies change. This needs one small addition to your proxy — see apps-script-currency-addon.gs.txt in the project files. With it installed, Fetch all financials sets the currency for your entire list in one pass, and the toast tells you what it found.

A company already fetched keeps whatever currency it was given until it's fetched again — so after installing or fixing the currency addon, plain Fetch all financials won't touch anything it already has, wrong currency included. Use Force refresh all instead to redo everything already pulled.

Until then there's a built-in fallback, applied only where nothing is recorded yet: BHP RIO S32 WDS STO FMG ANN JHX AMC CPU QBE RMD BXB CSL CRN SQ2 NEM KAR PRU SFR PDN as USD, and XRO as NZD. A starting point rather than gospel — worth checking the ones you actually hold, because a company wrongly marked USD has its multiple wrong by about half.

Either way you can set it yourself: every company has a Reports in dropdown under Identity. Blank means "not known", which is treated as matching the share price. A fetched currency always wins over the built-in list, and over anything you typed.

The three line items

  • COGS — direct cost of producing what gets sold. For a miner: diesel, explosives, crews, haulage, processing. Not head office.
  • SG&A — running the business rather than making the product. Corporate salaries, marketing, legal, audit, directors' fees.
  • D&A — the annual write-down of plant and intangibles. Non-cash, but subtracted here because gear wears out and an acquirer has to fund replacing it. That is the whole quarrel with EBITDA.

Build-up versus top-down

Build-up is Carlisle's original: revenue less COGS, less SG&A, less D&A. It works on US filings because the income statement is presented by function.

Top-down is usually the only workable route on the ASX. Under AASB 101 most Australian companies present expenses by nature — employee benefits, raw materials and consumables used, depreciation and amortisation — so no COGS or SG&A line exists in the statutory accounts. Take EBIT and add back the one-offs.

What counts as a one-off

  • Restructuring and redundancy provisions
  • Impairments of goodwill, mine assets, mastheads
  • Gains or losses on selling a business or property
  • Legal settlements and remediation charges
  • Acquisition and demerger transaction costs

Do not add back anything that shows up every year. A company that impairs something annually is telling you that is a cost of doing business.

Where this breaks on the ASX

  • Banks, insurers and REITs never rank. Debt is their raw material, so enterprise value means nothing. The chip just controls whether you see them.
  • Miners and energy get a cyclical flag. A 4× multiple at the top of an iron ore or coal cycle is a peak-earnings trap, not a bargain. Check it against average earnings over a full cycle.
  • Half-year reporting. Most ASX companies report twice a year. For a trailing twelve months figure, add the latest half to the prior full year and subtract the matching half.
  • Franking credits sit outside this calculation entirely.

Units

Every dollar figure in millions, except share price in dollars, and shares on issue in millions.

Using it properly

Cheap multiples cluster around businesses in trouble. This ranking tells you where to start reading, and that is all it is for. Carlisle's own approach was a basket of thirty-odd rebalanced annually, not a bet on the cheapest name.

Nothing here is financial advice — it is arithmetic, and the arithmetic is only as good as the figures you feed it. Verify against the actual filing before you act.

The strategy
  • Own the cheapest names by Acquirer's multiple, equal weight.
  • Top up monthly. A first purchase in any stock has to clear the ASX $500 minimum, so early on the money opens two positions rather than dusting all of them.
  • Review quarterly. The only reason to sell is a company dropping out of the target — never how long you've held it, never how far it has run.
  • Sell whole positions, never part of one.
  • If something drops out while in profit, holding to a year and a day halves the taxable gain, so wait where that is still ahead. At a loss there is nothing to wait for.
